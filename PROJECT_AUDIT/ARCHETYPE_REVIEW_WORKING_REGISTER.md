# Instinct 13 — Archetype Review Working Register

**Status:** Working document; non-normative until reviewed and approved by the project owner.  
**Purpose:** Review all twelve archetypes without treating inherited names, pairings or animal illustrations as unquestionable canon.

## Review order

For each archetype pair, work in this order:

1. **Concept:** What human pattern, tendency or mode of being does each archetype represent? Describe observable characteristics without moral ranking.
2. **Opposition:** What is the meaningful tension between the two concepts? Confirm that they are genuine counterpoints, not merely different animals or unrelated traits.
3. **Name:** Choose a concise name that expresses the concept. Do not use the animal's name as a substitute for the archetype concept.
4. **Animal / symbol:** Select an animal only after the concept and name are sufficiently clear. Treat the animal as a replaceable metaphor, not as the archetype itself.
5. **Visual manifestation:** Define visual language only after the conceptual work. Do not let legacy artwork dictate the concept.
6. **Decision record:** Record the rationale, alternatives considered, owner approval, date and version in the Decision Log.

## Review principles

- An archetype is a conceptual pattern, not an animal.
- An animal is a symbolic representation and may change independently.
- Names may change when they misrepresent the concept.
- Pairings may change if the conceptual opposition does not hold.
- No archetype is inherently better, more evolved or morally superior to its opposite.
- Avoid definitions that assume a fixed endpoint or universal human classification.
- Preserve uncertainty explicitly; do not fill gaps by inference.
- Do not force a relationship between the twelve archetypes and the separate DQD dimensions.
- The 13th remains outside the twelve: formless, nameless and not an animal or archetype.

## Pair 5 — current working notes

The project owner confirmed that The Performer and the transformation archetype are opposites.

The transformation concept, as currently described by the project owner:
- limitations are often internal to the individual;
- learning and experience produce continuous evolution;
- transformation does not erase the past or rebuild from absolute zero; it incorporates and reorganizes what came before;
- a change of perspective can reveal a different meaning or possibility without requiring external reality to change;
- transformation is not necessarily moral improvement, a superior state, or a predetermined final form.

**The Becoming** is a candidate name only. It is not approved as final. The animal symbol remains undecided. The butterfly is not the archetype's name and is not the 13th manifestation.
