# Instinct 13 — Six-Pair Review Progress

**Status:** Active working document; no pair definitions in this register are approved merely by appearing here.  
**Purpose:** Identify recurring qualities and contextual manifestations across the twelve archetypes without closing their meaning, classifying people or treating legacy labels as complete accounts.

## Confirmed system structure

- The twelve positions are **archetypes**: recurring patterns of human experience.
- Each archetype can manifest in different ways according to person, context, experience and time.
- A person is not an archetype and must not be permanently classified by one. The twelve archetypes can alternate in their expression within a person or across situations; pairing does not imply that a person is fixed to one side or that the two sides are mutually exclusive.
- Each archetype has one animal as its current symbolic representation; the animal is not the archetype and may be changed independently if the concept warrants it.
- The twelve are organized as six opposing pairs.
- The 13th is not one of the twelve and remains nameless, formless and without an animal or character representation.

## Review method

Each pair is reviewed in this order:

1. Identify recognizable qualities and signals for both archetypes without relying exclusively on animal metaphors.
2. Explore the differences, tensions and complementarities between them without assuming strict binary opposition.
3. Identify multiple possible manifestations for each pattern, without turning examples into rigid diagnostic criteria.
4. Test whether the names communicate the concepts accurately.
5. Review animal symbolism only after the concepts are stable.
6. Check overlap with the other five pairs and avoid moral ranking or a simplistic good/evil split.
7. Record owner-approved observations separately from working hypotheses; do not convert observations into exhaustive definitions.

## Iterative deconstruction and reformulation method

The review is consensus-driven and deliberately non-confirmatory: an initial answer is a hypothesis to examine, not a conclusion to defend. The review must deconstruct the answer, reformulate the question and answer again.

For each archetype and each pair, apply this cycle:

1. **Initial question:** State the conceptual question being investigated, with scope and terms made explicit.
2. **Initial answer:** Record the first answer as a hypothesis, including its provenance (owner-authored, legacy source or assistant-generated). Do not upgrade its status by repetition.
3. **Deconstruction:** Challenge the answer. Identify hidden assumptions, ambiguous terms, unsupported claims, contradictions, counterexamples, cultural bias, moral ranking, overlap with another archetype and dependence on the animal metaphor.
4. **Reformulated question:** Replace the original question with a more precise or deeper question that addresses the weaknesses found.
5. **Revised answer:** Answer the reformulated question, explicitly explaining what changed and why. Preserve unresolved alternatives rather than forcing closure.
6. **Cross-pair coherence test:** Test the revised concept against the other eleven archetypes, all six pair tensions, the archetype/manifestation distinction and the constitutional role of the 13th.
7. **Provisional synthesis:** Record identified qualities, possible contextual manifestations, overlaps, remaining uncertainties, evidence and any decision that requires owner approval.

Repeat the cycle whenever a material contradiction, ambiguity or new piece of evidence appears. A cycle is not complete merely because a plausible answer has been written.

### Review outcomes

- **Confirmed:** The analysis withstands deconstruction and the project owner explicitly approves the concept/decision.
- **Provisional:** The current answer is coherent enough to retain as a working hypothesis, but analysis or owner approval remains outstanding.
- **Rejected:** The answer fails the coherence test, depends on unsupported assumptions or misrepresents the intended concept. Preserve the reason for rejection so it is not inadvertently reintroduced.

The objective is not to validate the first answer or produce a closed definition. It is to identify what can be recognized, challenge interpretations, reformulate questions and refine the observations. The process must remain open to overlap, alternation and context. Consensus means agreement on the method and explicit recording of decisions; it does not mean treating an assistant hypothesis as owner approval.

## Constraints for all pair reviews

- Do not reduce an archetype to its animal.
- Identify qualities and manifestations; do not turn them into exhaustive or restrictive definitions.
- Do not equate one behaviour with an entire archetype.
- Do not classify people as fixed types.
- Do not assume one side is good, evolved, healthy or superior and the other bad or inferior.
- Do not assume every pair is a simple binary or that the two sides exhaust human behaviour.
- Do not interpret an opposing pair as mutually exclusive states or permanent classifications. The twelve archetypes can alternate in their expression; the exact mechanics of alternation remain open for review.
- Do not force a mapping to DQD dimensions.
- Do not rename or replace the 13th.
- Distinguish owner-approved concepts from assistant-generated hypotheses and legacy labels.
